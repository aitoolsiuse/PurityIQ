## What the report itself says (verbatim, marked)

Statements safe to paraphrase on slides (no banned words), reproduced from the Letter and Executive Summary — these are general statements about the whole 2024 program (all commodities, including almonds), not almond-specific findings:
- "In 2024, more than 99 percent of the commodities sampled through PDP had residues below the established EPA tolerances, with 42.3 percent having no detectable residue." (Letter, printed p. ii, PDF p. 2)
- "Pesticide residues in food are expected to be at or below the tolerance if the pesticide is applied following the label directions." (Letter, printed p. ii, PDF p. 2)
- "Tolerances are established only after EPA has determined that foods containing these residue levels meet the regulatory standard of reasonable certainty of no harm." (Letter, printed p. ii, PDF p. 2)
- "Because PDP data are used for risk assessments, PDP laboratory methods are geared towards detecting very low levels of pesticide residues, even when those levels are well below the tolerances established by EPA." (printed p. vii, PDF p. 7)

Verbatim quote that contains a banned word (do NOT reuse on slides or caption; kept here for accuracy only): "The data reported by PDP illustrate that residues found in agricultural products sampled are at levels that do not pose risk to consumers' health and are safe according to EPA and FDA." (Section V.A, printed p. 20, PDF p. 30)

## Notes

- ams.usda.gov/datasets/pdp was not re-fetched as a live page this session; the PDF was pulled directly from the URL above, which the PIQ-010 sourcing note already confirmed is the current, newest annual summary listed on the AMS reports page. The file downloaded cleanly (3.68 MB, 213 pages) and was converted to text in full with pypdf; nothing in the PDP data tables was inaccessible.
- The candidate commodities suggested in the task brief (strawberries, spinach, kale, grapes, green beans, bell peppers, baby food) were checked against Table 1 ("PDP Commodity Collection Schedule for 2024," printed p. 7) and are NOT part of the CY2024 sampling cycle in this report — they appear only in the report's "Look Ahead" section as commodities scheduled for the 2025 survey (printed p. 24), which has not yet been published. The actual CY2024 commodities are: almonds, apples, avocado, blackberries (fresh and frozen), cherry tomatoes, cucumbers, salmon, head lettuce, leaf lettuce, onions, oranges, pineapple (fresh and frozen), potatoes, canned pumpkin, sweet corn (fresh and frozen), and tomatillos. Almonds was picked from this actual list because it is not apples, is not one of the "other commodities" already listed with numbers in the PIQ-010 sourcing note (avocado, onions, head lettuce, cherry tomatoes, tomatillos, blackberries, cucumbers, leaf lettuce, oranges, potatoes, canned pumpkin, sweet corn, pineapple), is a common household pantry/lunchbox/snack item parents buy directly (not just an ingredient), and has a clean, surprising, well-quantified detection-rate finding.
- "87.6 percent had at least one pesticide detected" is arithmetic on the printed Appendix I row (100 minus 12.4). The report prints both the percentage and the underlying sample count (66 of 531) for the zero-detection column, so this derivation is checked against real counts, not just the rounded percentage.
- "Detected" means a lab instrument found a residue at or above that pesticide's limit of detection in a composite 1-pound almond sample. It does not mean the food "contains" a level of concern and does not mean harm. Almonds in this survey were tested whole and ground, not washed first (unlike apples, which were rinsed 15-20 seconds before testing) — so the almond results reflect the product as typically sold, not a post-rinse residue level.
- Not confirmed: the geographic origin (domestic vs. imported) split for almond samples specifically. The report gives this breakdown for apples (98.4 percent domestic) but does not give a parallel almond-specific origin percentage; only the no-tolerance-established almond findings are marked domestic in Appendix J. Do not state an almond origin percentage.
- Not confirmed: any health effect of the specific residues found in almonds; that is outside what the report itself says beyond the quoted EPA-tolerance framing above.
- Brand names: none used.
