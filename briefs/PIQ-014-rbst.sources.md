Notes
- Australia: multiple non-government sources describe rBST as unregistered/not used in Australia, but no primary Australian government source (APVMA or FSANZ) stating this directly was located in the time available. Per instructions, this is not included as a confirmed claim.
- The brief's assumption that Directive 2003/74/EC (amending 96/22/EC) governs the EU's BST rule is not correct: that Directive and its parent 96/22/EC concern anabolic/hormonal growth promoters and beta-agonists generally (e.g., oestradiol) and do not mention bovine somatotrophin. The actual controlling EU instrument for BST is the separate Council Decision 1999/879/EC (repealing Decision 90/218/EEC, the original 1990 moratorium). This was confirmed by reading both the Directive 2003/74/EC legislative summary and the BST-specific EUR-Lex summary page.
- No primary-source figure was found for how common "rBST-free" labels are on retail milk cartons today (as opposed to farm-level bST usage). The 2002/1996 USDA NAHMS figures above measure bST use on dairy farms, not the prevalence of "rBST-free" label claims at retail; more recent (post-2003) USDA/NAHMS bST-usage figures were sought but not confirmed from a primary document in the time available, so only the 2002/1996 figures are reported. This data is dated (23+ years old) and flagged as such.
- eCFR/Federal Register redirect pages that pointed to "unblock.federalregister.gov" were not followed, consistent with instructions; the govinfo.gov copy of the 1994 Federal Register notice was used instead.
- FDA's own bST product-safety page (fda.gov) does not itself contain the milk-composition-equivalence statement in the form quoted above; that statement comes from FDA's 1994 Federal Register interim guidance document (govinfo.gov), which is the primary source for both the labeling rule and the composition statement.
