Notes
- Nothing in this file is drawn from news or blogs. Leads from search (NY bill, Utah, Arizona, Illinois, Arkansas, Maryland) were followed only to the legislature pages above.
- 21 CFR 172.730 is the only regulation titled "Potassium bromate", and it is limited to malt. Use in flour and bread is permitted through the standards of identity (137.155, 136.110), not through 172.730. Do not write "FDA approves potassium bromate as a food additive for bread" unless worded as "FDA standards of identity permit it in bromated flour and bread." I did not find an FDA statement in a primary source that resolves whether FDA considers flour use to be grandfathered; the FDA pages fetched do not say. Do not claim it.
- eCFR and Federal Register were not used (redirect to an unverified host). CFR text is the 2024 edition on govinfo (dated 2024-04-01). I did not verify a 2025 or 2026 amendment to 137.155, 136.110 or 172.730. FDA's list page (current as of 07/29/2026) shows most recent action "NA", which is the currency check. Treat "unchanged since the 2024 CFR edition" as likely but not directly proven for the CFR text.
- EU: absence is confirmed for the Union list in Reg 1333/2008 as consolidated 18.08.2026. I did not fetch an EFSA opinion on potassium bromate, and I did not confirm any EU statement of why it is not listed. Do not attribute a reason. Frame as divergence: "not on the EU list of authorised additives; permitted in US bread flour under FDA standards."
- Prop 65 date: the OEHHA PDF says the date is the initial appearance on the list. OEHHA's chemical page was blocked (bot challenge), so the listing basis and any NSRL were not checked. Separately, "Bromate" (CAS 15541-45-4) is listed as cancer, May 31, 2002; that is a different entry. Only claim the potassium bromate row.
- AB 418 is a sales and distribution ban on food that "contains" the substance, effective 1 Jan 2027, four substances. Today is before that date, so use future tense ("takes effect Jan 1, 2027"). AB 418 does not have a "detected" standard; it says "contains".
- Label check: the ingredient list must declare each ingredient (137.105). I did not find a primary-source rule fixing the exact word on the label beyond that (the standardized name is "bromated flour"; the additive is potassium bromate). A parent can scan the ingredient list for either wording, but do not claim products are required to use one specific phrase. Products using no bromated flour will not list it; absence from the list is the check.
- Not confirmed (excluded): any IARC classification (IARC page did not load), any FDA petition to revoke or ban (none found in FDA pages fetched), Arizona HB 2164, Illinois SB 93, Arkansas SB 9, Maryland HB 1208, and any other state law (not fetched from legislature pages), what share of US bread uses it, and any health-effect claim beyond the Prop 65 "cancer" listing.
