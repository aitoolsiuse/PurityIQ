Notes
- Not confirmed and left out: the EU hazard/CLP classification of BHA or BHT. ECHA's substance-information and brief-profile pages (echa.europa.eu) returned HTTP 403 to a direct fetch; the CLP Regulation's own Annex VI (eur-lex, Regulation 1272/2008) could not be retrieved because eur-lex reports the document as too large (22.3 MB) to display in the fetch tool. Search leads (not cited as sources) suggest BHT has no EU harmonised classification and only aggregated industry self-classification (aquatic hazard) in ECHA's C&L Inventory; IARC (a WHO body, not an EU government source) classified BHA Group 2B in 1986 based on rodent forestomach tumors. Per the task's instruction to use an EU government source only for this specific check, and per the verification standard ("if a claim can't be confirmed from a primary source, cut it"), neither claim appears in the table above.
- Not confirmed and left out: the EFSA re-evaluation opinions on BHA (2011, EFSA Journal 2011;9(10):2392) and BHT (2012, EFSA Journal 2012;10(3):2588), including their ADI figures. efsa.europa.eu redirects both to efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com, which returned HTTP 403 to a direct fetch. Search-result summaries describe a raised BHA ADI (1.0 mg/kg bw/day) and a BHT ADI of 0.25 mg/kg bw/day with no genotoxicity concern, but these were not confirmed by reading the actual opinion, so they are not cited as sources.
- Not confirmed and left out: Indiana HB 1137 (2026, introduced 1/5/2026, would add BHT — not BHA — to a 13-item school food ban). This bill has not passed as of today, and the fetch of its introduced-bill PDF did not return readable text, so it is not included as a confirmed source. It is a lead only.
- eCFR and federalregister.gov's normal document pages were not used because both redirect to an unverified host (unblock.federalregister.gov); the two FDA Requests for Information were instead read from public-inspection.federalregister.gov, the Office of the Federal Register's own pre-publication text service, and from fda.gov.
- No final rule, proposed rule, or Federal Register action revoking or amending 21 CFR 172.110, 172.115, 182.3169, or 182.3173 was found as of today; both BHA and BHT remain fully authorized under the CFR 2024 edition text quoted above, and both are currently the subject of open (BHT) or closed-but-unresolved (BHA, comment period ended in April 2026) Requests for Information only, not proposed changes to the regulations themselves.
- The EU Annex II entries for E 320 and E 321 quoted above come from Commission Regulation (EU) No 1129/2011, which is the regulation that originally populated Annex II to Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008. A search for later amending regulations mentioning "E 320" or "E 321" (2020 onward) found none; the base Regulation 1333/2008 itself is still shown as consolidated as recently as an 18 Feb 2026 EUR-Lex version in search results, but the full current consolidated text and its Annex II could not be directly fetched (the fetch tool returned empty content for the ELI/consolidated pages, apparently due to document size), so currency of the exact 2011 entry is inferred from the absence of a contrary amendment, not from reading a current consolidated Annex II directly.
- "GRAS" and "food additive" are two separate, overlapping US authorizations for BHA/BHT: 21 CFR 182.3169/182.3173 (GRAS, 0.02% of fat/oil basis, general antioxidant use) and 21 CFR 172.110/172.115 (food additive, specific ppm limits per named food). Both remain in force; the FDA RFI documents cite both pathways for each substance.
- Word "safe" appears in verbatim CFR/FDA quotes above (GRAS = "generally recognized as safe"; FDA RFI text "if BHA/BHT remains safe under its current conditions of use") and is quoted, not used as our own characterization.
