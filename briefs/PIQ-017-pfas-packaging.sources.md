Notes
- Neither California's AB 1200 food-packaging provision nor New York's S8817 requires any consumer-facing label disclosing PFAS content on a wrapper, bag, or take-out container. Both are point-of-sale prohibitions on non-compliant packaging, not disclosure/labeling regimes. (AB 1200 does add a PFAS-disclosure and label requirement elsewhere in the same bill, but that provision applies to cookware, not to the "food packaging" article covered by this story — confirmed from the bill text, which contains the food-packaging ban in one article and references DTSC/the designated-chemical list for cookware in a separate article.)
- A claim reported by several secondary/law-firm sources — that California's Department of Toxic Substances Control must adopt enforcement regulations by 1 July 2029 and begin enforcement by 1 July 2030 — could not be confirmed from AB 1200's own bill text (the specific provision was not found there) and is not included above per the verification standard.
- echa.europa.eu returned HTTP 403 to every direct fetch attempt in this session (same behavior noted for a different domain in the PIQ-002 file). The ECHA page content cited above was retrieved by rendering the live page through an alternate fetch path, not from a search snippet or a secondary summary.
- eur-lex.europa.eu would not render for the PPWR regulation text itself (Regulation (EU) 2025/40, CELEX 32025R0040) with any fetch method tried; content came back empty. The PPWR facts above are instead confirmed from the European Commission's own environment.ec.europa.eu news release — an official EU government source, published the day the rule took effect — rather than from EUR-Lex directly. The exact PFAS limit values (mg/kg) inside the PPWR text were not independently confirmed from a primary source and are deliberately omitted.
- No PFAS content disclosure was found required on any food packaging label, federal, EU, or state. A parent cannot check a wrapper or take-out box for a "contains PFAS" or "PFAS-free" line the way they can check a Nutrition Facts panel; "PFAS-free" marketing claims exist in the marketplace but no government-run certification or standard label scheme for them was found in this research.
