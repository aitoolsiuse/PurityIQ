Notes
- Citation check (per the PIQ-006 method): the user's working citation, Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/2108, is real and is the regulation that lowered EU nitrite/nitrate limits — confirmed by fetching the EUR-Lex text directly rather than assuming it was correct. No wrong-citation problem found this time.
- Category 08.3.4 (traditional cured products such as Wiltshire bacon, jamón curado, presunto — immersion-cured and dry-cured) also appears in this regulation with its own before/after numbers, but a second, targeted fetch of the Annex only returned verbatim text for categories 08.3.1 and 08.3.2. The 08.3.4 numbers were reported by the same tool in a first, broader summary pass but were not independently re-verified against quoted annex text, so they are left out of the table above rather than stated as confirmed.
- Could not obtain the exact Official Journal (OJ L) citation and precise entry-into-force date beyond "the twentieth day following [OJ] publication" — a follow-up fetch to pull the OJ reference returned empty content. The regulation is dated 6 October 2023; the two application dates that matter for a slide (9 October 2025 for the meat categories above) are directly quoted from the annex text and are solid.
- fsis.usda.gov, usda.gov, and regulations.gov returned HTTP 403 (blocked) on every attempted page in this session, including pages on "uncured" labeling policy, celery powder/celery juice as a natural nitrate source, and bacon-specific guidance. ecfr.gov and federalregister.gov searches redirected to unblock.federalregister.gov, which per the rules for this task was not followed. As a result: the widely-referenced FSIS labeling-policy requirement that an "uncured" product using celery powder must carry a disclaimer such as "No Nitrates or Nitrites Added Except for Those Naturally Occurring in Celery Powder" could NOT be confirmed from a primary source this session. The CFR text that IS confirmed (9 CFR 317.17) requires the "No Nitrate or Nitrite Added" statement but does not itself mention celery powder.
- Also could not confirm, from a primary source, any FSIS or FDA action or petition since 2024 specifically on nitrite/nitrosamine formation in cured meat, for the same access reasons (fsis.usda.gov, regulations.gov blocked; federalregister.gov redirect not followed). This should be treated as unconfirmed, not as evidence no such action exists.
- The commonly cited "18% increased colorectal cancer risk per 50g of processed meat daily" figure is about processed meat generally (the IARC 2015 evaluation), not about nitrite/nitrate specifically, and must not be presented as if IARC evaluated sodium nitrite itself.
