Notes
- Frame as divergence, not "stricter means better": California requires a warning above a set exposure level; FDA has reviewed the same chemical and, as of today, has not set a limit or required labeling, saying it has no reason to believe current exposure levels pose an immediate or short-term risk.
- Do not claim 4-MEI is present in any specific named brand's product -- no brand-level testing data was checked. The story is about the caramel-color class generally and the label/warning-requirement gap, not about which sodas currently contain how much 4-MEI.
- "Class III and IV caramel coloring" is the source's own wording (the ammonia-process and ammonia-sulfite-process types most associated with 4-MEI); Class I and II caramel colors do not use ammonia and are not the subject of the Prop 65 listing. Slide copy should not use "Class III/IV" jargon -- say "the process used to darken cola-colored drinks" or similar plain wording if needed, or just "caramel color" generically since the label itself does not distinguish classes either.
- Coca-Cola and PepsiCo reportedly reformulated to reduce 4-MEI in California-sold product after the 2012 safe-harbor regulation took effect (found in secondary reporting, e.g. Lexology/BeverageDaily/FoodNavigator coverage from 2012-2014); this was not independently confirmed against a primary source and is NOT used as a claim in the brief -- the brief sticks to the current regulatory gap (state warning requirement vs. no federal limit), which is independently confirmed and still live today.
