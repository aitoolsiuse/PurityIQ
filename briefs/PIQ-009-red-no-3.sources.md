Notes
- The words "risk" and "safe" appear only inside verbatim FDA quotes (marked). The FDA page and the Federal Register order both use them; slides and caption must not.
- Framing: the EU has NOT banned erythrosine. It authorizes E 127 for a narrow set of cherry products only. The accurate divergence is: the US allowed it in many foods (candy, cakes, cookies, frozen desserts, icings) until the 2025 order; the EU lists it for cocktail, candied and bigarreaux cherries only. FDA also states other countries "still currently allow for certain uses."
- The 15 Jan 2027 date is the order's "effective" date, when the food listing is removed. FDA's own page calls it the date manufacturers have "to reformulate." FDA does not use the term "compliance date" in the order text fetched.
- The FDA page (content current as of 01/15/2025) was fetched but is dated; the 2026 status change comes from the Federal Register notice of 5 Aug 2026. A search of the Federal Register API for "Red No. 3" after 17 Jan 2025 returned only that one document.
- The FDA constituent update says FDA's estimate is that Red No. 3 is "not as widely used" as other certified colors; do not imply it is in most foods.
- "Sell-through": the order says food made while the certificate was effective is not adulterated because of the color. It does not give a retail cutoff date; do not state one. California AB 418 separately bars selling or holding for sale a food containing Red 3 from 1 Jan 2027.
- eCFR and Federal Register pages were not used; the order and the 2026 notice were read from govinfo.gov. Federal Register API was used only to find the document numbers.
- FDA cosmetics: the 1990 action is described in the 2025 order as FDA "did not extend the provisional listing" for cosmetics and externally applied drugs. It is not stated there as a "ban"; use FDA's wording.
