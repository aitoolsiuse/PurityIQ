Notes
- Quotes above marked verbatim may contain words on the banned list (only the 21 CFR 184 subchapter heading and the AB 418 short title). Do not put those on slides or in the caption.
- EU wording: the EU "withdrew" E 216 and E 217 from its authorized additive list (Directive 2006/52/EC). The stated reason is EFSA's inability to set an ADI for propylparaben because of a missing clear NOAEL, after effects on sex hormones and male reproductive organs in juvenile rats. Say "EFSA could not set an acceptable daily intake"; do not say EFSA found propylparaben harms children. Methyl and ethyl parabens (E 214, E 215, E 218, E 219) remain on the EU list. Frame as divergence, not as one system being better.
- The EFSA 2004 opinion itself (EFSA Journal 2004;83, "Opinion of the Scientific Panel on food additives, flavourings, processing aids and materials in contact with food (AFC) related to para hydroxybenzoates (E 214-219)") returned HTTP 403 on efsa.europa.eu and the journal host, so it was not read directly. The recital in Directive 2006/52/EC quotes its conclusions, and that is the cited source. A 2006 or 2015 EFSA opinion on propylparaben was not located or confirmed, and is not cited.
- eur-lex.europa.eu returned an access challenge to automated requests; the same OJ and consolidated texts were read from the EU Publications Office (publications.europa.eu, Cellar), which serves the identical published documents. EUR-Lex ELI links are given for readers.
- Reg (EU) No 1129/2011 did not itself remove E 216 and E 217; the removal dates to 2006/2008. Do not attribute the removal to 1129/2011.
- CFR text is the 2024 and 2025 print editions from govinfo.gov (eCFR was not used). Federal Register or eCFR redirects to unblock.federalregister.gov were not followed. No FDA notice changing 21 CFR 184.1670 appears in the 2025 edition (current as of 1 Apr 2025). A later 2025-2026 amendment cannot be fully excluded from govinfo alone; FDA's July 2026 list page shows no such action.
- FDA's petitions-under-review table (dated 19 Aug 2026) loads its rows dynamically and could not be searched, so the absence of a propylparaben petition is NOT confirmed.
- A label saying "propylparaben" does not mean it exceeds any limit; FDA's rule says the level is limited by good manufacturing practice, with a stated maximum level of 0.1 percent in food. Do not imply the products are harmful; California's ban starts 1 Jan 2027 and applies to selling food containing it.
- No brands named. "Detected vs contains": this is a label (ingredient) story, not a residue-test story.
