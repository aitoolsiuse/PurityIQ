Notes
- EUR-Lex's own site blocks plain HTTP fetches with a JavaScript bot-check (AWS WAF challenge); the EU citations above were retrieved by loading the pages in a real, cookie-bearing browser session (Claude in Chrome), not by curl or the plain WebFetch tool, which both returned empty content. No unblock.federalregister.gov-style third-party redirect was used or needed for any source in this brief.
- 21 CFR / eCFR (ecfr.gov) was avoided per instructions; all US regulatory text is quoted from govinfo.gov CFR annual-edition PDFs (Title 7, Chapter I), cross-checked against the 2024 and 2025 editions where noted, plus the live ams.usda.gov pages for current-status confirmation.
- The brief's assumed exact phrase "regardless of whether or not there is DNA or protein from the genetic modification detectable in the final product" was NOT found verbatim. The closest primary-source language is Recital 21 of Regulation (EC) No 1829/2003 ("irrespective of the detectability of DNA or protein resulting from the genetic modification in the final product"), which is preamble language explaining the labelling policy, not an operative legal requirement itself — the operative rule is the 0.9% threshold in Article 12(2). Any slide using the "detectability" framing should paraphrase Recital 21, not claim it as an article, and should not overstate it as a direct EU vs US contrast, since the US's own detectability standard (7 CFR 66.9) is the more precise mirror-image comparison.
- No brand names appear in any source excerpt above.
