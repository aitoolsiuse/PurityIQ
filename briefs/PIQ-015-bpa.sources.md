| EU: Commission Regulation (EU) 2024/3190 (adopted 19 Dec 2024, published OJ L series 31.12.2024) prohibits the use of BPA and its salts in the manufacture of adhesives, rubbers, ion-exchange resins, plastics, printing inks, silicones, and varnishes/coatings intended for food contact, and bans placing on the EU market any food contact material made using BPA; materials made with a substitute bisphenol "shall not contain any residual BPA." Two narrow exceptions remain (large >1,000-litre tank coatings and filtration membranes) where "Migration into food shall not be detectable." Regulation entered into force 20 days after publication, i.e. 20 January 2025. It repeals and replaces Regulation (EU) 2018/213 | Commission Regulation (EU) 2024/3190 of 19 December 2024 (EUR-Lex, full text, Articles 1, 3, 4, 13, 14 and Annex II) | https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R3190 | 2026-09-27 |
| Basis for the 2024 EU ban: EFSA's 2023 opinion set a new tolerable daily intake (TDI) for BPA of 0.2 nanograms per kilogram of bodyweight, "20 000 times lower" than the temporary TDI of 4 micrograms per kilogram (4,000 ng/kg) EFSA had set in 2015; EFSA "concluded that there is a health concern from dietary BPA exposure for all population groups" | Commission Regulation (EU) 2024/3190, recitals 3-4 (EUR-Lex) | https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R3190 | 2026-09-27 |
| Transition periods under the 2024 EU ban: single-use food-contact articles made under the old rules may still be placed on the market until 20 July 2026 (18 months); articles for preserving fruit, vegetables or fish, and exterior-only metal-can coatings, until 20 January 2028 (36 months); repeat-use professional food-production equipment already on the market may stay in use until 20 January 2029 | Commission Regulation (EU) 2024/3190, Articles 11-12 (EUR-Lex) | https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R3190 | 2026-09-27 |
| Prior EU rule (now repealed): Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/213 of 12 February 2018 set a specific migration limit of 0.05 mg of BPA per kg of food for BPA used in plastics, varnishes and coatings, and separately banned any detectable migration of BPA from varnishes/coatings on materials meant to contact infant formula, follow-on formula, processed cereal-based baby food and similar infant/young-child products. Status shown on EUR-Lex: "No longer in force, Date of end of validity: 19/01/2025, Repealed by 32024R3190" | Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/213 (EUR-Lex) | https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2018/213/oj/eng | 2026-09-27 |
| France: Loi n° 2010-729 (30 June 2010), as modified by Loi n° 2012-1442 (24 December 2012), suspends the import and "mise sur le marché" (placing on the market, paid or free) of any food packaging, container or utensil containing BPA intended for direct food contact. The ban on items for infants/young children took effect the first day of the month after the 2012 law's promulgation, i.e. 1 January 2013. The text of Article 1 (version in force 27 Dec 2012 - 19 Sept 2015) states this suspension "prend effet...au 1er janvier 2015 pour tout autre conditionnement, contenant ou ustensile" — takes effect 1 January 2015 for all other food-contact packaging, containers and utensils | LOI n° 2010-729 du 30 juin 2010 (Article 1, version en vigueur du 27/12/2012 au 19/09/2015), Legifrance | https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/LEGIARTI000026832119/2012-12-27 | 2026-09-27 |

Notes
- Labels: no primary source found requiring BPA to be disclosed on a US food label. FDA's own BPA pages describe no consumer label mandate, and a 2015 bill that would have required BPA labeling on cans (the "BPA in Food Packaging Right to Know Act") did not become law — this bill is noted only as a lead, not cited as current law, and is not included as a claim above because it never took effect.
- "BPA-free": no US government definition of the claim "BPA-free" was found on fda.gov, govinfo.gov, or ecfr.gov. This is cut per the verification standard rather than asserted from secondary sources.
- eur-lex.europa.eu, oehha.ca.gov and www.legifrance.gouv.fr blocked plain HTTP fetches (bot-protection screens with no readable text); their content above was read through a live, JS-rendered browser session instead of the fetch tool, still landing on the same government URLs shown.
- Per the task rules, redirects from federalregister.gov to unblock.federalregister.gov were not followed; the one Federal Register document needed (FR Doc. 2022-14682, the 2022 BPA petition filing notice) was instead retrieved as a PDF directly from govinfo.gov.
- CAP tracker id used for the pending BPA petition is FAP_2B4831 (not the TiO2 tracker id CAP_3C0325 used in PIQ-002 — different chemical, different docket).
