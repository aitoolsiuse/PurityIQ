Notes
- eur-lex.europa.eu could not be fetched in this session (the consolidated Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008 pages returned as empty/unrenderable, likely a JS-rendering issue with this fetch tool); EU authorization status and the phenylalanine labelling requirement are instead confirmed directly from EFSA's own pages, which are an acceptable primary source under the verification standard. No claim about the exact Annex II/Annex III citation text is made here since it could not be independently verified against eur-lex.
- FDA's ADI (50 mg/kg bw/day) and the international/EU ADI (40 mg/kg bw/day, from JECFA 1981 and EFSA 2013) are genuinely different numbers, both from primary sources. Both regimes currently permit aspartame; this is a difference in the numeric margin FDA uses, not a ban-vs-allow divergence, and should not be framed as "the US is looser" beyond what the numbers say.
- The word "safe" appears only inside quotation marks attributed to IARC, JECFA, FDA or EFSA above; it is not used elsewhere in this document.
- This story does not pass the classic "the EU banned X; the US allows it" divergence frame: both the US and EU currently authorize aspartame, and both bodies' 2023 statements affirm the existing ADI. See verdict for a fit-test discussion and an alternative honest framing.
